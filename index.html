What Is Islam?

Islam means submission to the One God. It is a way of life for about two billion people (roughly 26% of the world in 2020, according to Pew Research), from every country and background. Start here to learn what Muslims believe, what they do, and where those answers come from.

The short answer

Muslims believe in one God, Allah, who has no child and no equal (Quran 112). They believe God sent messengers before Muhammad, including Noah, Abraham, Jesus and David (Quran 4:163), and that Muhammad (peace be upon him) received the final revelation, the Quran.

The Arabic word Islam means submission to God, and a Muslim is one who submits. Muslims greet each other with as-salamu alaykum, "peace be upon you".

The Five Pillars of Islam

In meaning, the Prophet said: "Islam is built on five: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the prayer, paying zakat, Hajj to the House, and fasting Ramadan." Sahih al-Bukhari 8, Sahih Muslim 16

1. Shahadah

أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله وأشهد أن محمدًا رسول الله

The declaration of faith: there is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger. Saying it sincerely is how a person enters Islam.

2. Salah

إقام الصلاة

Five daily prayers at set times, facing the Kaaba in Makkah: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha.

3. Zakat

إيتاء الزكاة

Obligatory charity given from wealth above a minimum amount (the nisab), to the categories of people the Quran lists (9:60).

4. Sawm

صوم رمضان

Fasting from dawn until sunset during Ramadan, the ninth month of the Islamic calendar: no eating or drinking in daylight.

5. Hajj

حج البيت لمن استطاع إليه سبيلا

Pilgrimage to the Kaaba in Makkah, required of Muslims who are physically and financially able (Quran 3:97).

The Six Articles of Faith

When the angel Jibril asked the Prophet about faith (iman), he answered in meaning: faith is to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and in the divine decree (qadar). Sahih Muslim 8, sunnah.com

  1. Allah. One God, with no equal. See Surat al-Ikhlas below.
  2. His angels. Jibril (Gabriel), who brought the revelation to the Prophet, is one of them.
  3. His books. The Quran mentions earlier scriptures, including the Psalms given to David (4:163) and the Torah and the Gospel (5:46), and presents itself as the final revelation.
  4. His messengers. From Noah to Jesus to Muhammad, peace be upon them all (Quran 4:163).
  5. The Last Day. The day of judgment.
  6. The divine decree (qadar). Belief that everything happens with God's knowledge and will.

The Quran

The Quran is the word of God revealed to the Prophet Muhammad over about 23 years. It has 114 chapters (surahs), and it is read and memorized in the original Arabic. Before prayer: wudu (ablution)

  1. Make the intention in your heart and say "Bismillah".
  2. Wash both hands.
  3. Rinse the mouth three times.
  4. Sniff water into the nose and blow it out, three times.
  5. Wash the whole face three times.
  6. Wash the right arm from wrist to elbow three times, then the left.
  7. Wipe over the head, then wipe the ears.
  8. Wash both feet to the ankles three times, starting with the right.

Source: Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, How to perform Wudhu. Details vary slightly between schools of thought.

How the prayer is performed

  1. Stand facing the Kaaba (the qiblah) and make the intention (niyyah).
  2. Say "Allahu Akbar" to begin, then recite Al-Fatihah.
  3. Bow (ruku), then rise saying "Sami'Allahu liman hamidah".
  4. Prostrate (sujud), sit, then prostrate again. This completes one rakah.
  5. After the final rakah, sit and recite the tashahhud, then end the prayer with the salam (peace) to the right and left.

Condensed from Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, How to perform Prayer, which has the full words and positions.

Zakat, Ramadan and Hajj in more detail

Zakat

  • Due on wealth that reaches the nisab and has been held for a full lunar year (zakat.org).
  • The rate on gold and savings is 2.5%. The nisab for gold is 85 grams of pure gold (IslamQA). Some organizations use 87.48 g (Islamic Relief), so ask a scholar or your zakat body which figure to use.
  • The Quran lists who may receive zakat, including the poor, the needy, debtors and travelers in need (9:60).

Ramadan

  • The ninth month of the Islamic calendar. Muslims fast from dawn until dusk, eating and drinking only before sunrise and after sunset (Britannica).
  • The Quran was revealed in this month (2:185).
  • The same verse gives ease: the sick and travelers make up the days later.
  • The evening meal that ends the fast is called iftar.

Hajj

  • Held in Dhul Hijjah, the twelfth month of the Islamic calendar (Wikipedia).
  • Pilgrims enter a state of purity called ihram, wear special garments, and walk seven times around the Kaaba (Britannica).
  • Hajj is required of those who are physically and financially able, as long as their absence does not burden their family (Britannica).
  • Eid al-Adha falls on the 10th of Dhul Hijjah (Wikipedia).

The Life of the Prophet Muhammad

Key dates according to the traditional account. Early dates are approximate. Source: Wikipedia, Muhammad.

  1. c. 570

    Birth in Makkah

    Born in Makkah into the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe.

  2. 610

    First revelation

    At age 40, in the Cave of Hira near Makkah, the angel Jibril (Gabriel) appeared to him and the revelation of the Quran began.

  3. c. 613

    Public message

    He began to spread the message of Islam publicly to all the people of Makkah. His followers faced persecution for 13 years.

  4. 622

    The Hijrah

    Migration from Makkah to Madinah. This year is the start of the Islamic (Hijri) calendar.

  5. 624 to 628

    Madinah years

    The battles of Badr (624), Uhud (625) and the Trench (627), and the ten-year truce known as the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (628).

  6. 630

    Conquest of Makkah

    The Muslims entered Makkah.

  7. 632

    Farewell pilgrimage and passing

    He performed his farewell pilgrimage and passed away in Madinah.

The Rightly-Guided Caliphs

The first four leaders of the Muslim community after the Prophet, called the Rashidun ("Rightly Guided"). Their combined rule lasted 29 years. Source: Britannica, Rashidun.

Abu Bakr

632 to 634

The first caliph. His rule began with the wars of the riddah, tribal uprisings in Arabia (632 to 633).

Umar

634 to 644

Under the Rashidun the community expanded into Iraq, Syria, Palestine, Egypt, Iran and Armenia. Umar introduced the Hijri calendar in 639.

Uthman

644 to 656

The Rashidun period established an authoritative reading of the Quran.

Ali

656 to 661

His caliphate ended with the first Muslim civil war (fitnah, 656 to 661).

The Islamic Calendar

The Hijri calendar has 12 lunar months, each beginning around the new moon. A year has 354 or 355 days, so the months move through the seasons. Years are counted from the Hijrah in 622. Source: Britannica, Islamic calendar.

How does someone become a Muslim?

Through the Shahadah, the declaration of faith, which is the first pillar of Islam. It is said with sincere belief in its meaning.

أشهد أن لا إله إلا الله، وأشهد أن محمدًا رسول الله

"I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah."

For guidance on next steps, such as learning to pray, speak with a local mosque or a qualified teacher.

Quran 2:256: "There shall be no compulsion in religion." Faith has to be a free choice.

Common questions

Do Muslims worship Muhammad?

No. Muslims worship God alone. The Shahadah itself states that Muhammad is the Messenger of God, and it places God first.

What do Muslims believe about Jesus?

The Quran calls Jesus (Isa) the Messiah, the son of Mary, and a messenger of God, honored in this world and the next (3:45). It also says that God is one and is exalted above having a son (4:171).

Is Islam only for Arabs?

No. Only about 1 in 5 Muslims live in the Middle East and North Africa, according to Pew Research. The Quran addresses all of humanity (49:13).

Why do Muslims pray five times a day?

The prayers are spaced through the day so that one is constantly reminded of Allah and given chances to seek His guidance (Dar al-Ifta).

Does Islam force people to convert?

The Quran states "There shall be no compulsion in religion" (2:256).
